## Quiet stadium ambience

`public/audio/stadium-ambience.m4a` is an edited loop from **noise#01.aif** by **huubjeroen**, released under CC0 on Freesound. The author describes a crowd/pitch microphone mix from a Feyenoord match at De Kuip, Rotterdam. It is real stadium audio, not synthesized noise.

- [CC0 1.0 Universal](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/)

The source page and CC0 dedication were checked on 8 September 2026. CC0 permits modification and redistribution, including commercial use; this source note retains provenance without implying endorsement.

The edit selects seconds 126–158 of the 324.944-second source. This interval had the lowest combined average level and level variation among the measured 32-second windows. A two-second overlap blends the end into the beginning, producing a 30-second stereo loop. Peak scaling to 0.70 precedes 44.1 kHz AAC encoding at 128 kbps. The final file is 473,431 bytes; decoded peak is 0.6962 with zero clipped samples. The measured loop-boundary difference is below the recording's ordinary adjacent-sample variation. This is a numerical continuity check, not a listening assessment.

Source MP3 SHA-256: `476935dd27a03ed88f13ec1df224d26e3814ce21011207eaf6239ea391c365ee`.

Edited AAC SHA-256: `362b1631db9391673c28d9a80385df7ee3424d3d2ef0b047e6ff9bbadbffa08f`.

The loop plays only during visible, enabled 1× playback, at gain 0.045 before the user's master volume. At the default 0.45 volume its measured average level is about −59 dBFS. A gentle entrance keeps it in the background. Pause, mute, seek, speed changes, hidden tabs, full time and disposal stop it; resuming chooses an offset from recording time. Loading never starts audio by itself. All synthesized drums and percussion, including save/miss and celebration-landing accents, have been removed following the user's listening feedback. The existing goal roar remains the distinct foreground crowd response.

## Stadium goal roar

`public/audio/goal-roar.m4a` is an edited excerpt of **Football-crowd-GOAL.wav** by **paulw2k**, from Freesound. The author describes recording a home goal at Brentford’s ground during the 2006/07 season, with the club’s permission to record around the ground. The previous studio teenagers/applause asset has been removed.

- [Original sound and author description](https://freesound.org/people/paulw2k/sounds/196461/)
- [Freesound’s HQ MP3 used for this edit](https://cdn.freesound.org/previews/196/196461_755728-hq.mp3)
- [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

The source page and license were read on 8 September 2026. Credit, source/license links and the edited-excerpt notice also appear under **Inside the match → Rules**. No endorsement by the author or football club is implied.

The source MP3 is 50.947 seconds. The edit takes 4.4–11.5 seconds, starting at the natural eruption. It retains stereo and original pitch, scales the PCM peak to 0.82, adds a 35-millisecond entrance and a 1.65-second fading tail, and encodes 44.1 kHz AAC at 144 kbps. The resulting asset lasts 7.1 seconds. No repeated loop, synthetic voices or additional applause are mixed into it.

Source MP3 SHA-256: `41466ad6d261e1f7e1346e250781ef2fd908e6e519a5ea7f53c94ed915cad5d0`.

Playback loads and decodes the local file once after the Play gesture. It uses a modest scoring-supporter pan and reduced gain. A late download never replays a past goal; an unavailable asset leaves the whistle.

Source selection used the author’s description and the recording’s measured amplitude envelope. Decoding, duration, clipping/headroom and playback lifecycle were checked. No agent listening assessment is claimed; the in-app match is the listening preview.

## Original synthesis

Ball contacts, post sound and referee whistles are generated by this project’s `src/audio/stadium-sounds.ts`. Their sample output remains unchanged. No model call or external audio service is used. Synthetic percussion, a continuous noise bed, harmonic crowd voices and procedural claps are absent. The real stadium recordings may contain natural applause and spectator whistles.

The user approved the whistle while listening to the development preview. Only terminal whistles survive automatic playback end; their volume remains adjustable without continuous Canvas drawing.
